Proof-support paths

A set of assumptions cannot tell two proofs apart. The module therefore makes the proof itself an object, and binds every verified status to an exact one.

Implemented Named proof-support paths, path identity, and the separation of structural support from established status are implemented and covered by the repository tests.

What a support path is

A SupportPath is a tree: the supported statement, the root derivation, and the path taken through every premise, down to primitive leaves. TheoremGraph.support_paths returns every surviving proof of a statement, formed as the whole Cartesian combination of its premises' proofs in a fixed order, minimising nothing. possible_dependencies is the union of the assumptions those proofs carry and necessary_dependencies their intersection.

Path identity

Path identity is the content hash of the whole tree together with the semantic digest of every record each node rests on: the supported statement, the derivation that concludes it, its active assumptions, and every binding it cites. The whole digest is used rather than a prefix, because this identity is what a verified status is granted against.

  • Two proofs with identical assumptions are still two proofs.
  • Revising any record under its own identifier ends the proofs that rested on the old meaning.
  • Statement status is excluded from those digests, so promoting a statement does not rename the proofs beneath it.
  • Open obligations are excluded too: readiness is a question about the current graph, not about which proof this is.

Structural support and established status

A statement has structural support when some proof survives, and it is established when some surviving proof also owes no open obligation. The two are reported apart, which is what lets an invalidation say that support remains while the work to re-establish it is open.

The two realized-support projections

Which projection a consumer reads is decided by what that consumer holds.

ProjectionKeyed byRead by
selected_supports(derivation_id) A derivation identifier, then the deterministic selection policy. Graph admission, verifier_premise_projection, and instantiated_assumptions.
path_supports(SupportPath) The explicit path it is given, including one the policy did not choose. path_premise_projection, which is what the commit boundary adjudicates against.

Both apply the same realization order, the same support identity rule, and the same realization checks. Neither returns a list that carries a proof identity: the identity is the supplied path's, and the candidate and the certificate are what carry it. No consumer of either projection walks the proof records its own way.

How the realized support list is ordered

Every cited premise comes first, realized through the binding that instantiates it; then the derivation's own undischarged assumptions, proof-local first; then, per cited premise, the exported and carried conditions the premise's selected proof still owes, transitively, realized through the same binding. Each entry carries its role, the identifier of the record it came from, the binding that produced it, its kind, and the realized formula with the realized domain.

Two entries are the same entry only when the role, the origin identifier, the kind, the realized formula, and the realized domain all agree. One condition reached through two premises therefore appears once, while two distinct records that share a formula, one record under two kinds, and one record realized through two different bindings each stay two.

One active-edge predicate

TheoremGraph.is_active_derivation is the single predicate that decides what takes part in active proof, and every active check asks it and nothing else: cycle rejection, realized-support admission, binding source-scope compatibility, proof-local ownership, discharge, path enumeration, and selection. A proof is active when it is valid and every root it rests on is active.

A record that is not an active edge stays in the graph as canonical provenance of a proof that was once offered. It keeps every reference and identifier rule, but it constrains nothing: it cannot close a cycle, its assumptions are not owed, and its realized supports are not required to fit a conclusion it no longer supports. Withdrawing something must not make the graph harder to repair than it was before.

Open limits, stated rather than implied

  • Every surviving proof is enumerated individually and the union and intersection of what they depend on are reported, but no lattice over them is modelled, and the enumeration is unbounded because the number of proofs is the product over a statement's premises. Recorded as D-14.
  • The earlier selection policy, selected_derivation and selected_supports, is still public. Nothing decisive uses it, but a future caller could reach for it where an identity is required. Recorded as D-15.
  • The active-edge predicate is recomputed on every call, with no index, because a graph holds nothing but frozen record tuples. Recorded as D-20.
