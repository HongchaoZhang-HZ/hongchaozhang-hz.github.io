Revision and recovery

Meanings change. The module treats a revision as a recorded action at the same boundary that advances a checkpoint, and it never quietly rewrites history to accommodate one.

Implemented The revision seam, its gates, withdrawal and restoration, the causal resume link, segmented replay, and authenticated recovery are implemented and test-backed. Concurrency and coordination between independent writers remain out of scope.

One generic seam

RevisionRecord carries a source kind, a source identifier, the prior hash, either a revised hash or an explicit withdrawal or restoration marker, a causation identifier, a reason, and the checkpoint it was computed against. It imports nothing from any registry or notation package and has no case-specific branch.

A concept root is external, so it names the authority and authority snapshot it was computed against and must arrive with an ExternalWitness attesting to the same root, symbols, snapshot, and both semantic hashes. The witness is bound into the action hash.

Six gates, at the same boundary

state-authenticity
revision-freshness
revision-root
structural-admission
effect-closure
obligation-reopening

CommitAutomaton.apply_revision is the only boundary that may apply a revision. An action carries only the mutation its revision authorises, and a declared replacement hash is recomputed rather than believed. A replacement proof must keep the conclusion of the proof it replaces. Every refusal returns the caller's own state object, so a failed invalidation leaves no partial effect by construction rather than by cleanup.

What a revision does to proofs

An invalidation demotes a statement only when the last verified proof is lost. A verified proof is lost when it stops surviving, and also when it still exists but rests on the source that was revised, which is how an external concept reaches the graph at all. Structural support and verified status therefore diverge explicitly, rather than leaving a statement marked verified on the strength of a proof that no longer exists.

The effect record is computed against the complete final graph, including the demotions and the reopened work, so its revised graph hash and surviving path identifiers are those of the state actually committed. An existing obligation of an affected derivation is reopened with its cause recorded and its discharging evidence dropped rather than rewritten out of history. A new obligation is created only when a statement lost its verified status, still has structural support, and its surviving proof carries no obligation at all.

Withdrawal is not deletion

RootStatusRecord records whether a statement or binding root still takes part in active proof. The record stays addressable and keeps its history, and every proof that consumed it stops being an active edge. Only the commit boundary writes one, activity is a sequence rather than a flag, and a restoration must find the withdrawal it claims to reverse and name it.

Resuming work afterwards

After a committed invalidation the affected Hypothesis stream may append a causally linked successor. Two event types carry it, revision-committed and work-resumed, and the projection records the revision, the effect, the candidate being superseded, and the checkpoint the successor pins. All four are part of the projection hash, so a successor answering a different invalidation is a different candidate. Prior events are never rewritten: replay still distinguishes the stale candidate from its successor.

Recovery

Snapshot segmentation, authenticated recovery, single-read public carriers, and append idempotency are implemented, and exact-boundary recovery is covered by the segmented replay tests. They do not coordinate competing writers and do not provide a distributed consensus layer. Nothing here coordinates two writers.

A statement revised under its own identifier is demoted rather than re-adjudicated. Deciding whether the old evidence happens to cover the new meaning would require running the verifier inside the commit boundary, which no round has authorised. Recorded as D-17. Whether a named external authority told the truth, and whether the party presenting an attestation is that authority, cannot be checked here and is not claimed; that is D-16, and the registry integration itself belongs to a round that is not started.
