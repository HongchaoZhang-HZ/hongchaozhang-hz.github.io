Purpose and boundary

Theorem Derivation records what a project is entitled to treat as established, keeps that apart from what was merely attempted, and lets exactly one interface advance the committed state.

Implemented Rounds 01 through 07 of the vertical slice are implemented and passed their recorded review and regression gates. Rounds 08 through 10 are not started. The package uses the Python standard library only.

The one-sentence boundary

The module is a vertical slice: a thin but complete path from a proposed claim to a committed checkpoint, with every step recorded. It is not a general theorem prover, and this documentation does not claim it is one. The bundled adapter enumerates declared finite domains, the external SMT seam reports unsupported because no solver is configured in this repository, and no solver is installed, emulated, or invoked here.

What it owns

  • A records and the B theorem graph hold committed theorem state. The graph is the single authoritative statement store and it is a frozen value: every extension returns a new graph and revalidates the whole structure, so a refused transition cannot leave a partial write behind.
  • The C attempt ledger records what was tried. It never determines what holds.
  • The verifier plane produces scoped evidence about one target at a time.
  • The commit automaton is the only boundary that advances a checkpoint. Every non-advanced outcome returns the caller's own state object, so the prior checkpoint is preserved by construction rather than by a comparison after the fact.

Those four lines are the whole authority story, and the organization architecture page draws them twice: once across the repository's modules, and once inside this one.

What it refuses to own

  • It does not decide human questions. A recorded review changes governance status only, and mathematical verification status and human governance status are separate fields with disjoint vocabularies.
  • It does not rewrite the Hypothesis Work Unit. A theorem outcome does not change a Hypothesis status, and publication never edits a human-owned Hypothesis package.
  • It does not admit notation. No adapter imports a Notation V1 snapshot into a theorem case, and no theorem record carries a checked Notation V1 or V2 record identifier.
  • It does not coordinate writers. Concurrency and coordination between independent writers remain out of scope.

What Rounds 01 through 07 cover

The implemented and test-backed rounds cover formal records, the theorem graph, the attempt ledger, proof-support paths, semantic invalidation, segmented replay and recovery, verifier and adapter contracts, authenticated promotion, checkpoint lineage, and the deterministic Obsidian and Herdr projection. Snapshot segmentation, authenticated recovery, single-read public carriers, append idempotency, and deterministic read-only views are implemented.

Status language on these pages. Implemented behavior is described in the present tense. A current human procedure is called a manual step. A missing adapter is called not implemented, and it is drawn as a dashed arrow rather than left to look finished. Rounds 08 through 10 are not started, so nothing on these pages describes them as available.

The bundled case is an example, not a result

The example case states one claim under four formulations, and the four land on four different outcomes: one refused by a replayed witness, one advanced over a finite declared domain, one indeterminate when the same formulation is stated over an unbounded quantity domain, and one unsupported because it asks for a solver capability nothing provides. The published round artifacts are reproducibility evidence, not theorem-state authority.
