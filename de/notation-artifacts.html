Canonical artifacts

Each artifact family records one kind of claim. Together they are the semantic record; the rendered tables are only a view of it.

Implemented These artifact families are defined by the published schemas and are validated by the repository tests.

The families

FamilyWhat it records
Source recordsA source that material came from.
EvidenceThe recorded material that supports a claim about meaning.
Local symbolsA symbol as a specific source writes it, with its declared or inferred meaning.
Semantic conceptsThe meaning itself, with definition, scope, assumptions, and type or unit information.
Project symbolsWhat the project writes for a concept.
MappingsThe proposed relation between a local symbol and a concept, including how well scope, assumptions, and type align.
ConflictsA recorded disagreement rather than a silently chosen winner.
Review decisionsAn independent decision about a proposal.
Invalidation recordsWhat a revision changed and what it forces downstream.
Direct-alias claimsA deliberate claim that a source symbol simply is a project symbol.

Why direct-alias claims exist

The last family is unusual: it exists so that the shortcut the system forbids can still be written down. Someone who believes a source symbol is the project symbol can state that claim explicitly, and the gate can reject it with structured evidence. An unstatable claim would simply reappear as an undocumented habit.

Reviews are append-only with respect to meaning

Review decisions accumulate. A later decision does not erase an earlier one, so the record of how a concept came to mean what it means stays readable. Combined with invalidation records, this is what lets a reader reconstruct why a symbol changed, not just that it changed.

The generated tables under the index area render admitted records for human reading. They are views. The artifact families above are the record.
