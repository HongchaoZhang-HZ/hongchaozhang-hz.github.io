Architecture and roles

The Notation Backbone is a set of small modules with one shared rule: only admission writes to the registry, and only the writer renders the published view.

Implemented The modules below exist in the published package and are exercised by the repository test suite.

Modules and responsibilities

AreaResponsibility
Models and artifact schemasDefine every artifact kind and its required fields.
RegistryHold admitted records and keep referential integrity across them.
Proposal envelopesBundle staged records with the claim being made about them.
GatesNine independent checks that produce structured pass or fail evidence.
Admission transactionRun the gates and write only if the required gates pass.
InvalidationSeparate semantic revisions from non-semantic ones and open review when needed.
SnapshotsHash the concept, notation, and mapping layers separately.
Atomic writerRender the official view, validate it, and swap it into place.
WorkspaceOwn the on-disk layout that people actually look at.
Loading DockScan, hash, record, and route incoming files.
StagingHold proposed material that is not admitted state.
Command lineExpose the workflow as explicit commands.
Manifest-driven casesReproduce a described case in a clean target.

Role separation

Four roles are kept apart on purpose:

  • Generator proposes staged records only. Proposing is not admitting.
  • Critic reviews independently, and cannot be the proposer or the submitter of the package under review.
  • Human accepts declared concept-level semantic changes. Meaning is not promoted without that acceptance.
  • Deterministic writer renders admitted records and never invents meaning.

The independence requirement is enforced, not merely recommended: the critic gate checks that the review came from someone other than the proposer and submitter, so a single actor cannot approve its own proposal by writing both halves.

The workspace people see

The workspace is the visible file boundary. It opens with a Loading Dock and continues through numbered areas for sources, evidence, local notation, concepts, project notation, mappings, conflicts, reviews, indexes, snapshots, and templates. Machine state lives separately from the visible layout.

Generated Obsidian tables in the index area are views of admitted records. They are not the complete semantic record, and editing a rendered table is not a way to change meaning.

Nothing staged in the visible directories is admitted state; see Loading Dock and admission for the boundary that separates the two.
